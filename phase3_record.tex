\hypertarget{how-to-read-this-document}{%
\section*{How to read this document}\label{how-to-read-this-document}}

This document is kept as a laboratory notebook rather than a specification. It has two parts and one rule.

\textbf{Part I --- the frozen baseline and the decision set as it stood at the freeze} (§1--§19). Part I was written before any Phase 3 implementation, against repository tip \texttt{476c2a0}. \textbf{Part I is never rewritten.} Its claims about package behavior are claims about \texttt{476c2a0} and remain true of \texttt{476c2a0} regardless of what the package does now. Its ``Target'' columns, proposed defaults, and open questions are preserved as the record of what was intended and what was undecided at that moment --- including the parts that were later decided differently.

\textbf{Part II --- the amendment record} (A-1 onward). Every change of fact, decision, or status after the freeze is a numbered, dated, commit-stamped amendment. Part II is \textbf{append-only in the following precise sense}: an entry is opened once, on a dated occasion, and thereafter may only be \emph{appended to} --- by a dated continuation inside the entry, or by a later entry that supersedes it and says so. Text already written is not deleted or rewritten. Several entries below carry more than one date because they were continued in this way; that is the intended operation, not an exception to it. A correction to an entry is itself an appended paragraph, marked as a correction, never an edit of the sentence that was wrong.

\textbf{The rule.} Part I text that a later amendment has overtaken is \emph{marked in place, not corrected in place}. Four marks are used:

\amdref{A-7} & The adjacent statement is still accurate as a statement about the freeze tip, but Part II amendment A-7 records what happened to it afterwards. Read both. \\
\amd{A-7} & The row or item carries text amended in place by A-7 --- the amending text follows the tag. \\
\amdnew{A-7} & The row or item did not exist at the freeze and was added by A-7. \\

\end{longtable}

Marked blocks in the amendment colour, set off by rules, are amendments inserted \emph{inside} a frozen section where an in-line tag would not carry enough text.

\textbf{Why this form.} The failure mode this document exists to prevent is a refactor that quietly changes numerical or statistical results. That protection depends on the pre-implementation record staying legible after the implementation exists --- a document silently updated to agree with the code cannot testify against the code. Consequently: no \emph{unmarked} statement of current status appears in Part I. Marked amendment blocks inside Part I do carry status, which is the point of marking them; what Part I contains nowhere is an unmarked sentence whose truth depends on today's date. Status statements in Part II are always dated and stamped with the commit they describe. Reading the document at any future date requires only reading further into Part II, never re-reading a revised Part I.

\textbf{Companion records.} Per-phase acceptance evidence lives in the repository under \texttt{refactor-patches/\allowbreak{}phase3\{a,c,d,e\}/\allowbreak{}rebaseline\_record.md} and the tip-verification and reaudit records named in Part II. Part II summarizes and points; it does not duplicate them.

\hypertarget{polygon-diagnostic-specification}{%
\subsection{13. Polygon diagnostic specification}\label{polygon-diagnostic-specification}}

\textbf{Purpose:} quantify the rectangle-vs-polygon excitation-mass discrepancy on the Philadelphia district geometries to set the polygon-mode accuracy target and advise when rectangle mode is adequate. It does not decide whether polygon mode exists (D-17).

\textbf{Inputs:} district polygons (external dependency, §6.18); event locations per district; σ grid spanning representative values ≈ 10--500 m (log-spaced, e.g., 10, 20, 50, 100, 200, 500 m).

\textbf{Computation, per district and per σ:} for each event location s\_j (or a boundary-stratified subsample), the rectangle spatial mass M\_rect(s\_j; σ) --- the per-axis erf product of §5.12, optionally window-clipped --- and a high-accuracy polygon reference M\_poly(s\_j; σ) (adaptive quadrature or dense-grid summation with convergence check). Report as a \textbf{function of σ}, not at a single posterior estimate --- α and most of the temporal factor scale out of the relative comparison.

\textbf{Per-district outputs:}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  median and maximum spatial-mass difference M\_rect − M\_poly;
\item
  relative excitation-compensator overcharge Σ(M\_rect − M\_poly)/ΣM\_poly;
\item
  fraction of events within 1σ, 2σ, 3σ of ∂A;
\item
  variation across districts (identifies districts that fill their bounding boxes poorly);
\item
  cost of the high-accuracy polygon reference (wall time, memory);
\item
  the accuracy required of a production polygon-mass method (tolerance such that the induced compensator error is dominated by the declared cutoff ε and float tolerance).\amdref{A-9}
\end{itemize}

\begin{amendment}{A-9 --- the accuracy target this bullet asks for}{2026-07-21}
Discharged \emph{analytically}, not from the diagnostic. Under D-21 the omitted spatial mass is \(\epsilon_s = 1 - \operatorname{erf}\!\left(w_s / (\sqrt{2}\sigma)\right)^{2}\); at the preregistered reference \(w_s = 3\sigma\), \(\epsilon_s = 5.3923032 \times 10^{-3}\). Reserving a tenth of that already-accepted error for the numerical mass evaluation gives \(\tau_{\mathrm{abs}} = 5.39 \times 10^{-4}\), which is exactly this bullet's criterion --- numerical error dominated by the declared cutoff --- made numerical. The gate was fixed \emph{before} the backends were compared. Full derivation, the finite-cutoff form \(\min(\tau_{\mathrm{abs}}, 0.1\,\epsilon_s(\sigma))\), and the separate provisional derivative gate are in A-9. What the \emph{diagnostic} decided is a different question: when rectangle mode is defensible at all (A-8).
\end{amendment}

\textbf{Consumers:} OP-9 (numerical method + accuracy target); NUTS runtime expectations (baseline cost profile in §10.g); user guidance for mode selection; 3d test tolerances.

\begin{amendment}{A-8 --- §13 executed; results}{2026-07-21, \texttt{906b30b}}
Run on 18 Philadelphia planning districts (127{,}590 events) and 6 park polygons. \(M_{\mathrm{rect}}\) is the per-axis erf product of §5.12; the reference \(M_{\mathrm{poly}}\) is a divergence-theorem line integral (Gauss--Legendre order 20, edge step ≤ 0.5σ, 8σ clip with neglected mass < 2.5×10⁻¹⁵), self-tested against the analytic rectangle case and convergence-checked to ≤ 10⁻¹¹ under step halving and order 32. Events were boundary-stratified with inclusion weights; events at least 8σ from ∂A were handled analytically.

\textbf{Headline result --- the answer is regime-dependent, and that is the finding.} Relative excitation-compensator overcharge on \emph{planning districts} is ≈ 1--7\% at σ ≤ 50 m, rising to 8--39\% at σ = 500 m (Lower South worst). On \emph{ribbon parks} it reaches 50--650\%: rectangle mode is not defensible there at any σ in the tested range. The pre-freeze expectation --- that districts are miles across while excitation is tens of metres, so the rectangle approximation is probably adequate --- is confirmed \emph{for districts at small σ} and refuted for elongated geometries, which fill their bounding boxes poorly. Mode selection is therefore a per-geometry judgement, which is the substantive argument for D-23 (no default).

\textbf{Data defect found upstream:} three of the six park geometries were invalid and required \texttt{make\_valid} to process; flagged upstream, not repaired in the package. This discharges the §6.18 external dependency.

\textbf{Artifact caveat.} Per-region/σ report tables are written under \texttt{output/}, which is \texttt{.gitignore}d; the numbers above are therefore recorded \emph{here} as the durable statement, with input hashes recorded in the OP-9 shootout \texttt{run\_config}. The diagnostic module and its import-time self-test are tracked and rerunnable.
\end{amendment}

\hypertarget{a-2}{%
\subsection{A-2 --- Phase 3a: data contracts and unique membership}\label{a-2}}

\textbf{Opened} 2026-07-20; extended 2026-07-24 and 2026-07-27. \textbf{Commits} \texttt{3e69836}, \texttt{48f7d10}, \texttt{70c8677}, \texttt{f0b4375}, \texttt{f372e93}, \texttt{6e63efc}, \texttt{511ca5b}, \texttt{c22aa51}, \texttt{adea3d3}, \texttt{2cf326d}. \textbf{Class} IV + MR. \textbf{Amends} §5.1, §6.1--6.4, §10.a, §19.

\textbf{Conceptual basis.} A point-process likelihood has no defence against a malformed event: the event term credits it and the compensator debits a region, and if the two disagree about where the event is --- or whether it exists --- the disagreement is absorbed silently into the parameter estimates. The contract layer exists to make the boundary of the model explicit and to fail there, before any grid, membership join, or integral has been constructed. Placing it \emph{before} grid construction is the whole point: a violation named at the boundary is a data statement, whereas the same violation surfacing downstream is a symptom (\texttt{sjoin} produces the wrong number of rows) whose cause has to be reconstructed.

\textbf{Anchor correction to §5.1/§6.1.} The frozen text records ``NaN rows silently dropped at ingestion, \texttt{main.py:603}''. Reading the code establishes that \texttt{main.py:603} is the \emph{held-out scoring} path; the constructor never dropped NaN rows --- it crashed downstream in the membership \texttt{sjoin} with the misleading message about the computational grid. Both behaviors are closed, but the frozen characterization of which was which is corrected here rather than in place.

\textbf{Membership: why deduplication rather than arithmetic.} D-22 fixes the convention (left-closed, right-open per axis, outermost edges closed). The obvious implementation is to replace the spatial join with floor arithmetic on cell edges. That was rejected: floor arithmetic is a different computation, and near a cell edge it can differ from the join at ULP scale for events that are not on the edge at all. Instead the existing join is retained and its result deduplicated by maximum cell id. Because the field grid's ids are row-major from the bottom-left, the maximum id of an adjacent-cell tie \emph{is} the left-closed cell on an x-edge (+1), a y-edge (+25), or a corner (both), and events on the outermost right or top edge already join a single cell. Every strictly-interior event therefore passes through bit-identically, which is what the four golden pins confirm. On the covariate layer the same max-id rule is applied; on a row-major raster layer this coincides with left-closed, and on an arbitrary polygon layer it is recorded honestly as a deterministic convention rather than a geometric statement.

The temporal and seasonal axes were already D-22-compliant via \texttt{searchsorted(side='right')-1}; that is now pinned as contract rather than left as incidental behavior.

\textbf{Rebaseline scope.} No previously-working input changes result. The affected events --- on internal grid lines, on shared covariate edges --- could not be ingested at all before, or (on the held-out path) produced two \texttt{cov\_ind} rows with no length check, shifting every subsequent event's covariate assignment. The MR classification covers the transition from crash-or-corruption to deterministic membership, not a reassignment of valid events.

\textbf{Staged enforcement.} The layer landed report-only, preserving every legacy numerical path and legacy failure mode bit-for-bit, so that the §14 dry run (A-3) could inventory what would begin to fail. The default moved to \texttt{reject} only after that report was reviewed and signed off. \texttt{report} remains available as the dry-run instrument and is scheduled for removal (D-31).

\textbf{Later additions.} Finite positive horizon; polygonal, finite, positive-area domain geometry (previously a \texttt{LineString} domain produced empty domain checks and failed later as \texttt{event\_outside\_domain}, naming the wrong thing); and the CRS contract of A-16. A latent defect was found and fixed in the diagnostics themselves: the polygon-domain branch built its bounding rectangle without the transpose, so the internal-grid-line diagnostics were vacuous or wrong for every \texttt{GeoDataFrame} domain --- diagnostic-only, no rejection behavior changed.

\subsection{A-4 --- Phase 3b: the three-object seam}\label{a-4}}

\textbf{Opened} 2026-07-20. \textbf{Commits} \texttt{8084655}, \texttt{c6607ee}. \textbf{Class} BP. \textbf{Amends} §10.b. \textbf{Content amendment} in §10.b.

\hypertarget{a-5}{%
\subsection{A-5 --- Phase 3c: support on A, and the coverage contract}\label{a-5}}

\textbf{Opened} 2026-07-20. \textbf{Commits} \texttt{985c771}, \texttt{3120268}, \texttt{6d89b6e}, \texttt{1465e79}, \texttt{62ea1c1}, \texttt{e470cc0}. \textbf{Class} SC + IV + API. \textbf{Amends} §5.5--5.6, §6.5, §6.7--6.8, §10.c. \textbf{Content amendment} in §10.c.

\textbf{What was wrong and why it mattered.} On a polygon domain the frozen tip charged every in-domain field cell its full internal area \(1/625\) in the no-covariate background compensator. Cells were selected by intersection, so a cell that merely \emph{touched} \(A\) was charged a full cell, and boundary cells were charged their outside-\(A\) fraction. Separately, the background sampler drew locations over the full cell and relied on a downstream filter to discard points outside \(A\). The charged mass and the sampled support therefore described different regions, which is visible as overdispersion of the retained background count relative to its own compensator. The covariate leg had the same defect one level up: the refinement was built as \(C_c \cap A_m\) with no intersection against \(A\), and the covariate footprint --- not the declared domain --- decided the support.

\textbf{What replaced it.} One support object holds the clipped geometries \(C_c \cap A\) with their exact normalized intersection areas, and that same object feeds the likelihood integration arrays, the background location draw, and event-to-field-cell membership. For covariates the refinement is the exact triple intersection \(C_c \cap A_m \cap A\), verified against an independent shapely recomputation with total mass \(|A|\). \hypertarget{a-9}{%
\subsection{A-9 --- OP-9 resolved: backend and accuracy budget}\label{a-9}}

\textbf{Opened} 2026-07-21. \textbf{Commit} \texttt{62bde1f}. \textbf{Class} numerical method. \textbf{Amends} §13, §17; establishes D-23 and D-25.

\textbf{The quantity.} Polygon mode requires, per event \(j\) and per \(\sigma\),
\[
  M_j(\sigma) \;=\; \int_{A \,\cap\, C_j} \mathcal{N}\!\left(s - s_j;\, \sigma^2 I\right)\, ds ,
\]
evaluated inside NUTS, differentiably in \(\sigma\), for every event at every leapfrog step.

\textbf{Accuracy-budget derivation (settled).} Under the D-21 per-axis square cutoff the omitted spatial mass is
\[
  \epsilon_s \;=\; 1 - \operatorname{erf}\!\left(\frac{w_s}{\sqrt{2}\,\sigma}\right)^{\!2}.
\]
At the preregistered reference \(w_s = 3\sigma\) this gives \(\epsilon_s = 5.3923032 \times 10^{-3}\). Reserving 10\% of that cutoff-error budget for the numerical polygon-mass approximation gives
\[
  \tau_{\mathrm{abs}} \;=\; 5.3923032 \times 10^{-4} \;\approx\; 5.39 \times 10^{-4},
\]
which is the gate carried in the code. The logic is that the numerical error of the mass evaluation should be an order of magnitude below the error already knowingly accepted by truncating the kernel; a numerical method more accurate than that buys nothing, and one less accurate than that silently dominates a budget the user was told was the cutoff's. \textbf{This gate was fixed before the backends were compared}, and the comparison then selected a backend satisfying it --- not the reverse. For an actual finite cutoff the shootout used \(\min\!\left(\tau_{\mathrm{abs}},\, 0.1\,\epsilon_s(\sigma)\right)\), subject to the explicitly recorded numerical floor.

\textbf{The derivative gate is provisional and differently motivated.} \(\tau_{\mathrm{deriv}}\) was set equal to \(\tau_{\mathrm{abs}}\) as a separate numerical-stability criterion. It is \emph{not} derived from compensator truncation error, and it should not be read as if it were. Registered as OP-12.

\textbf{The comparison.} Three candidates were measured against the §13 oracle on five real geometries (up to 128 events each, deterministic boundary and interior selection, 49 \(\sigma\) values including off-grid and knot-adjacent, with a finite-difference derivative oracle whose own uncertainty is \(1.3 \times 10^{-7}\)).

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Fixed-node JAX boundary quadrature} is oracle-exact (\(\leq 2.1 \times 10^{-11}\); derivatives at the finite-difference floor) at every panel configuration --- and costs 28.5\,s per warm gradient on the largest polygon fit (\(n = 16{,}479\)), roughly \(4000\times\) the rectangle baseline, and fails outright in float32 through cancellation. Not viable as an in-sampler evaluator, but it is the right \emph{builder}.
\item
  \textbf{Oracle-built PCHIP tables} pass on values for \(K \geq 40\) and \textbf{fail the derivative gate at every \(K\)} (\(1.8 \times 10^{-2}\) at \(K = 64\)). This is the informative negative result: value accuracy and slope accuracy are separate properties of an interpolant, and a table that looks excellent by values alone would have fed corrupted gradients to NUTS.
\item
  \textbf{Quad-built tables} --- values and slopes taken from the independently validated quadrature backend, gated against the shapely oracle --- pass both gates on every geometry (at \(K = 64\): \(\leq 5.7 \times 10^{-7}\) on values, \(1.6 \times 10^{-5}\) on derivatives, zero bound violations), evaluate in 1.3\,ms at \(n = 16{,}479\) (about \(5\times\) \emph{faster} than the incumbent rectangle erf product), and cost roughly 23 minutes to build once per fit.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Decision.} One production path: C\textsuperscript{1} cubic Hermite lookup in \(\log \sigma\), \(K = 64\) log-uniform knots over the declared \(\sigma\) range, built offline by the fixed-node quadrature kernel in float64, with a loud no-extrapolation contract. The no-extrapolation contract is what makes the \(\sigma\) range a modeling commitment rather than an implementation detail, and hence what forces D-28 and D-29.

\subsection{A-10 --- Phase 3d: polygon excitation support modes}\label{a-10}}

\textbf{Opened} 2026-07-22; extended through 2026-07-25. \textbf{Commits} \texttt{edfce53}, \texttt{8c4a702}, \texttt{850a4b9}, \texttt{ae27947}, \texttt{8580364}, \texttt{238fbd3}, \texttt{0eeaebd}. \textbf{Class} CA + IV + \textbf{API}. \textbf{Amends} §10.d, §7.3; establishes D-26. \textbf{Content amendment} in §10.d.

\textbf{D-18 enforced structurally.} One \texttt{ExcitationSupport} object feeds offspring parenting eligibility and the compensator charge region, following the tip's single-sourced window predicate as the template. \hypertarget{a-11}{%
\subsection{A-11 --- Mass-table compatibility identity}\label{a-11}}

\textbf{Opened} 2026-07-24; extended 2026-07-25 and 2026-07-27. \textbf{Commits} \texttt{1adeb00}, \texttt{ce5508f}, \texttt{90d45bb}, \texttt{513cb1a}. \textbf{Class} IV. \textbf{Establishes} D-27.

A mass table is an event-indexed numerical artifact: row \(j\) is \(M_A(s_j, \cdot)\) for one specific event at one specific location under one specific domain and window. Reusing it against a different realization produces a likelihood that is silently wrong rather than loudly broken --- and if the event counts happen to match, nothing fails at all. Equal counts are therefore explicitly not treated as compatibility.

Validation covers the canonical domain-union geometry hash, event coordinates and row order, event count, realized spatial window, \(\sigma\) range and knot grid, interpolation convention, panel height after unit conversion, quadrature order, and backend schema version, plus array shapes and finiteness. Three strengthenings followed from reaudit:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Event identity is binary.} The original identity formatted coordinates with \texttt{.9g}, under which distinct float64 events collide. Identity is now a hash of little-endian float64 coordinates. Legacy artifacts and decimal-formatted identities are \textbf{intentionally rejected}; that incompatibility is deliberate and recorded, not a migration oversight.
\item
  \textbf{Provenance cannot be falsified.} Descriptive user metadata is nested under its own key and cannot overwrite reserved compatibility fields.
\item
  \textbf{Sidecar and artifact must agree.} Duplicated numerical and identity metadata are compared field by field against the archive payload before the table object is constructed, so a tampered or stale sidecar cannot disagree with the artifact and still load. \texttt{table\_id} remains descriptive and is explicitly \emph{not} treated as artifact-integrity verification --- stated so that nobody later mistakes it for one.
\end{itemize}

A related fix keeps ingestion honest about coordinates: real event coordinates are retained in float64 at ingestion, because the float32 affine round-trip through the internal axis scales can perturb a coordinate enough to change its hash, which would make a correctly prepared table appear incompatible with the model that prepared it.

\hypertarget{a-12}{%
\subsection{A-12 --- σ bounds and prior truncation}\label{a-12}}

\textbf{Opened} 2026-07-22; scope settled 2026-07-27. \textbf{Commit} \texttt{edfce53}. \textbf{Class} \textbf{SC}. \textbf{Amends} §7.3, §10.d; establishes D-28 and D-29; opens OP-10.

\textbf{What happens.} In polygon mode the user's \(\sigma^2\) prior is replaced by its exact truncation onto \(\left[\texttt{min\_sigma}^2,\, \texttt{max\_sigma}^2\right]\). Truncation is exact --- correctly normalized, samplable, batched, JIT-safe --- for the named families (\texttt{HalfNormal}, \texttt{LogNormal}, and their truncated forms); any other family raises rather than being silently approximated.

\textbf{Why it is SC and not a numerical device.} The Hermite table exists only over the declared \(\sigma\) range and refuses to extrapolate (D-25). Something must guarantee the sampler stays inside that range, and the only honest mechanism is to state that the prior has no support outside it. That changes prior support and therefore posterior support: it is a declared change to the model, required by the numerical method but not \emph{reducible} to it. Consequently the bounds must appear in model provenance, and the change carries semantic tests and a rebaseline record rather than a tolerance argument. \supsd{corrected 2026-07-28}{Where the supplied prior already has negligible or no mass outside the interval, truncation is behaviorally inert} --- but ``inert in this case'' is a property of the supplied prior, not of the mechanism.

\textbf{Correction appended 2026-07-28 (inertness requires containment, not smallness).} Truncation renormalizes: if the supplied prior places mass \(\delta > 0\) outside the interval, every retained density value is multiplied by \(1/(1-\delta)\) and the tails are cut. Exact behavioral inertness therefore requires that the supplied prior have \textbf{no support} outside \(\left[\texttt{min\_sigma}^2, \texttt{max\_sigma}^2\right]\), not merely little mass there. A prior with negligible outside mass makes truncation \emph{approximately} nonbinding, which is a claim about a particular prior and a particular posterior and is owed a sensitivity check --- not a general property that can be asserted at the interface. The distinction is exactly the one D-28 exists to protect: an SC change that looks inert is still an SC change.

\textbf{Bounds are use-case quantities (D-29).} A plausible range for an excitation length scale is a property of the phenomenon and the domain, not of the software. \texttt{min\_sigma} is accordingly required in polygon mode with no default. \texttt{max\_sigma} currently falls back to 5\,km converted through the projected CRS when omitted --- a convenience chosen for the expected Philadelphia district and park use cases, recorded in provenance, and \textbf{not} a scientific statement. Since that default silently sets the truncation interval of a prior, whether it should exist at all is registered as OP-10, and the present position is that both bounds should be required to be stated.

\hypertarget{a-13}{%
\subsection{A-13 --- Phase 3e: human units and computational cutoffs}\label{a-13}}

\textbf{Opened} 2026-07-22; extended 2026-07-24. \textbf{Commits} \texttt{2ce665c}, \texttt{5f5bb8d}, \texttt{a46c960}, \texttt{2a7c558}, \texttt{798f551}. \textbf{Class} API + CA + IV. \textbf{Amends} §10.e, §17; establishes D-24. \textbf{Content amendment} in §10.e.

\textbf{Interface.} The public temporal scale is \texttt{mean\_lag\_days}, supplied as a distribution in days and converted onto the internal \texttt{beta} sample site by \(\beta_{\text{internal}} = \texttt{mean\_lag\_days} \times (T_{\text{internal}} / T_{\text{days}})\); supplying both raises rather than resolving a precedence. Spatial quantities were already real-unit and were not reconverted, per the frozen §10.e --- with the qualification of A-12 that polygon mode nonetheless constrains the \(\sigma\) prior.

\textbf{Cutoffs as declared approximations.} Finite windows are computational cutoffs of infinite-support kernels, with omitted mass
\[
  \epsilon_t = e^{-w/\beta},
  \qquad
  \epsilon_s = 1 - \operatorname{erf}\!\left(\frac{w_s}{\sqrt{2}\,\sigma}\right)^{\!2}
  \quad \text{(per-axis square, D-21),}
\]
inverted for tolerance-based selection as \(w = \beta \ln(1/\epsilon_t)\) and \(w_s = \sqrt{2}\,\sigma \operatorname{erf}^{-1}\!\big(\sqrt{1-\epsilon_s}\big)\). Because \(\beta\) and \(\sigma\) are latent, selection uses declared \emph{design} values supplied by the user, and both the tolerance and the realized cutoff are recorded in \texttt{cutoff\_provenance}. The package defaults reproduce the historical rules of thumb exactly --- \(\epsilon_t = e^{-5}\) is \(w \geq 5\beta\), and \(\epsilon_s = 1 - \operatorname{erf}(4/\sqrt{2})^2\) is \(w_s \geq 4\sigma\) --- so the change is one of accounting, not of defaults: what was a rule of thumb is now a quantified omitted mass.

\textbf{Two audit corrections.} Tolerances are validated \emph{before} precedence is applied. Previously only the effective per-axis tolerances were checked, so a malformed shared tolerance was accepted whenever both axis-specific values overrode it, and an invalid tolerance behind a winning physical cutoff was retained in provenance instead of being rejected --- provenance recording a value the code never honored is worse than no provenance. And \texttt{set\_window} was made provenance-atomic and then fully transactional (A-14).

\textbf{Scope limit, deliberate.} \texttt{Temporal\_Power\_Law} has no tolerance or \texttt{mean\_lag\_days} interface, because its \texttt{beta} is a shape parameter and not a mean lag; inventing a conversion for it would be a modeling claim. An explicit physical window remains the compatible path for unsupported triggers.

\hypertarget{a-14}{%
\subsection{A-14 --- Transactional public state mutation}\label{a-14}}

\textbf{Opened} 2026-07-24. \textbf{Commits} \texttt{a46c960}, \texttt{798f551}, \texttt{8580364}. \textbf{Class} \supsd{A-19}{SC on the public API surface} → \textbf{CF + API} (reclassified 2026-07-28; see A-19). \textbf{Establishes} D-33.

\texttt{set\_window} mutated the window arguments and rebuilt the excitation pairs \emph{before} cutoff validation and support rebuilding completed. A rejected input or a failed rebuild therefore left an object whose windows and pairs had changed while its provenance and support had not --- an object that reports one cutoff and integrates another. That is precisely the silent-corruption mode Phase 3 exists to eliminate, and it lived in the public mutator.

The method now validates inputs, prepares pairs, cutoff provenance, excitation support, and (in polygon mode) the replacement mass table entirely in local state, and assigns only after the whole replacement succeeds. Provenance is re-resolved as a physical override of the realized windows, with the constructor restoring the original tolerance-selection record after initial installation so that how the first cutoff was chosen is not lost the first time a window is changed.

In polygon mode a temporal-only change reuses the installed table --- the table is indexed by events and spatial window, not by the temporal one --- while a spatial-window change requires a compatible replacement supplied by the caller, validated against the \emph{prospective} window before any mutation. Silent rebuild is forbidden here for the same reason as at construction (D-26).

\hypertarget{a-15}{%
\subsection{A-15 --- Held-out data as a standalone realization}\label{a-15}}

\textbf{Opened} 2026-07-23. \textbf{Commits} \texttt{8c4a702}, \texttt{8580364}. \textbf{Class} \textbf{CF} (see A-19). \textbf{Establishes} D-32.

\textbf{The scientific reading, stated so it is not misread as a train/test split.} Held-out data are evaluated as a \emph{complete standalone realization} over the model domain and horizon --- for example a different year at the same location. They are not a random subset of the fitted events, because removing a subset severs the parent structure the Hawkes term is defined by: an offspring whose parent was withheld is not a held-out observation of the same process, it is an observation of a different one.

\textbf{The reset qualification (appended 2026-07-28).} ``A different year at the same location'' qualifies as a standalone realization \textbf{only if the process is taken to reset at the year boundary.} In a continuing Hawkes process it does not: events late in the training year would excite events early in the held-out year, and that excitation is precisely what this score omits. State plainly what the implemented quantity is and is not. It \emph{is} the likelihood of the held-out realization treated as a fresh realization of the same process with the same parameters, self-exciting only within itself. It \emph{is not} the future-conditional likelihood \(p(Y_{\text{future}} \mid Y_{\text{train}}, \theta)\): it carries no training-to-test excitation and does not condition on the training event history. Where the held-out window genuinely abuts the training window, the reset is an assumption the analyst is making, and it should be stated with the score rather than inferred from it.

\textbf{The consequence.} All state derived from parent events must be rebuilt from the held-out realization: the excitation pairs, and in polygon mode the rows \(M_A(s_j, \sigma)\) of the excitation-mass table. Both the excitation event term and the compensator then use the held-out realization's own times and locations, and no training-event-indexed row is reused.

\textbf{What was wrong.} The held-out path rebuilt the pairs but reused the training-event mass table. With unequal train and test counts this crashed on shape; with equal counts it silently scored the held-out events against another realization's geometry-indexed masses. The fix builds a test-specific excitation support without mutating the training support or arguments. Rectangle mode is unaffected, since its mass is a closed-form function of the event coordinates rather than a stored per-event table.

This is a correctness requirement of the existing held-out likelihood. It does not change what that likelihood is intended to compute.

\hypertarget{a-16}{%
\subsection{A-16 --- CRS contract completed}\label{a-16}}

\textbf{Opened} 2026-07-24; completed 2026-07-27. \textbf{Commits} \texttt{c22aa51}, \texttt{adea3d3}, \texttt{e706107}, \texttt{2cf326d}. \textbf{Class} IV. \textbf{Amends} §5.2, §6.4; establishes D-34.

The real-unit trigger contract (D-15, §5.9--5.10) means that \(\sigma\), the spatial cutoff, and every distance in the excitation term are lengths in the coordinate system of the supplied data. A coordinate reference system that can be adopted, inferred, or overwritten without the user's knowledge therefore silently changes what those lengths mean. Four gaps were closed:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  a CRS-bearing domain with CRS-less covariates previously constructed, potentially adopting the domain CRS by assumption; it is now a named violation distinct from a mismatch;

\textbf{Correction appended 2026-07-28 (do not overstate what estimation buys).} An earlier draft of this entry said that estimating the amplitude ``removes the reason the fixed value had to be chosen''. It does not. Estimation replaces a fixed gain with a \emph{prior on} the gain, and thereby converts a calibration problem into an inference problem carrying its own obligations. Three of them are already visible. \textbf{(i) Identifiability:} the field amplitude trades off against the intercept \(a_0\) and against the latent field's own scale --- \(\exp(a_0 + \texttt{sp\_var\_mu}\cdot f_s)\) is invariant to compensating shifts --- and, through the excitation share, against \(\alpha\); a prior that does not break these ties will produce a ridge rather than an estimate. \textbf{(ii) The budget problem persists:} the unit-gain SBC scope was adopted because prior-predictive counts at production gain span roughly seven orders of magnitude and cannot be budget-bounded, and a \emph{prior} spanning that range has the same property. Estimation relocates the problem into the prior specification; it does not dissolve it. \textbf{(iii) Interaction with the known PriorVAE variance shrinkage} (§6.10) --- an estimated amplitude will partly absorb the decoder's understated second moment, which makes the estimate hard to interpret as a property of the field rather than of the decoder. This is therefore a \textbf{post-Phase-3 modeling decision requiring its own SBC stage}, not a settled item of deferred work, and it is recorded here as such rather than as a solved question.

\subsection{A-19 --- Change class CF added; A-14 and A-15 reclassified}\label{a-19}}

\textbf{Opened} 2026-07-28. \textbf{Class} DOC (taxonomy). \textbf{Amends} §7.2; reclassifies A-14, A-15.

\textbf{The gap.} §7.2's taxonomy has no class for the most common thing Phase 3's audit passes actually produced: a repair that makes the code do what it was already specified and believed to do. Such a repair is not BP --- something observable changes --- but it is not SC either, because SC is defined as a scientific/model-semantics change, and the successful-call posterior is untouched. Forcing these into SC inflates them and, worse, dilutes SC precisely where it must stay sharp: D-28's prior truncation is a genuine SC change, and it should not sit in the same class as a fix to a failure path.

\textbf{The class.} \textbf{CF --- correctness repair implementing the already-declared target.} Gate profile: a rebaseline record wherever any observable changes, plus semantic tests; behavior-preserving language remains forbidden; SC's sign-off requirement does not attach.

\textbf{Reclassifications.} \textbf{A-14} (transactional \texttt{set\_window}) becomes \textbf{CF + API}. On a successful call the resulting state is what the pre-existing contract already promised; what changed is the failure path, which previously left windows and pairs mutated while provenance and support stayed stale --- plus one success-path observable, \texttt{cutoff\_provenance}, which changed from stale to correct. \textbf{A-15} (held-out realization) becomes \textbf{CF}: D-32 states the scientific target the held-out score always had, and the repair makes the computation match it.

\textbf{Conflict recorded.} \texttt{refactor-patches/\allowbreak{}phase3e/\allowbreak{}rebaseline\_record.md} classifies the \texttt{set\_window} provenance atomicity as \textbf{SC}. This document now classifies it CF + API. The in-repo record is not edited by this entry; the disagreement is recorded here so that a reader who finds both does not have to guess which is deliberate.

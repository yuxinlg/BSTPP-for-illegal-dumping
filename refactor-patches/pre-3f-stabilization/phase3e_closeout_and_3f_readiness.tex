\section{The question, and the answer}

The question is whether to keep auditing until an audit returns no blocking issue, or to
adopt a different rule for opening Phase 3f.

The answer is: \textbf{do not open 3f yet, and do not keep running open-ended
whole-package audits}. Insert a bounded \emph{3e stabilization closeout} whose exit is a
finite, pre-registered readiness gate (\S5), and whose principal instrument is a
compatibility and state-transition matrix (\S6) rather than another general code review.
A clean blocker-focused audit \emph{is} required, but as the last step of a defined
protocol with a termination rule (\S9), not as an open standard of ``no defects
anywhere.''

The goal is not a defect-free package before 3f. It is a \emph{completely specified and
stable semantic surface} that 3f is permitted to rearrange.

\section{Why ``audit until clean'' is not a stopping rule}

\subsection*{Conceptually}

A stopping rule of the form \emph{stop when the instrument stops firing} is a statement
about the power of the instrument, not about the state of the artifact. Absence of
findings on pass $n$ is evidence that the auditor's search strategy has saturated. It is
evidence about the artifact only to the extent that the search strategy's coverage is
known --- and coverage has not been recorded, which is itself one of the gaps this
document closes (\S8).

Two structural consequences follow.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Correlated blind spots.} The same auditor re-reading the same modules
  returns nearly no information on the second re-read. \vfy{} at the close of the second
  Claude pass: \tt{\_sim\_cox}, \tt{preparation.py}, \tt{data\_contracts.py}, and any
  excitation-leg conservation check had \emph{not} been audited at all. A third pass over
  \tt{polygon\_mass.py} / \tt{excitation\_support.py} / \tt{cutoffs.py} would be nearly
  uninformative; a first pass over the unaudited set would be informative. Audit effort
  should be allocated by coverage, not by repetition.
  \item \textbf{The rule must be fixed before the evidence.} The register already
  practises this discipline elsewhere: \tabs{} was fixed \emph{before} the polygon-mass
  backends were compared, and the register says so explicitly, ``not the reverse'' (A-9).
  The same principle applies to the audit protocol. The 3f entry criterion must be pinned
  before the next pass runs, or the criterion is being fitted to its own findings.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Empirically, in this project}

The finding stream is not random, and its non-randomness is the actionable fact.

Across the two Claude passes (\vfy), the findings were: a sampler returning values outside
its own declared support; a domain area summed over rows while every consumer intersected
the union; a mass table silently rebuilt against a different realization; a public setter
rewriting the provenance of the axis it did not touch; a numerical default violating a
declared accuracy budget by roughly eighteen percentage points in the excitation
compensator at small $\sigma$; and one predicate with two spellings.

Every one of these violates an invariant the register had \emph{already declared} ---
D-27's compatibility contract, A-14/D-33's transactional discipline, A-21's
untouched-axis rule, A-21's \tabs{} budget, and \S11's single-sourcing reference pattern.
Not one of them had an executable check. A green suite of 427 tests coexisted with a fit
path that could not initialize at all.

\begin{ruling}
\noindent The defects are concentrated in the gap between the \emph{declared} register and
the \emph{executable} gate set, and specifically at combinations of options. That is a
statement about which instrument is missing. The missing instrument is a compatibility
matrix plus a register-to-test traceability map --- not another general review.
\end{ruling}

\subsection*{The cost of continuing to hold}

Holding 3f is not free, and two costs are worth stating.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Document divergence accumulates.} The objects 3f and 3g exist to
  reconcile are still diverging: conflict C6 (the guide's version identity is unpinned,
  so \S15's specified amendments have no pinned target) \infd; a superseded document
  still present in \tt{docs/} \vfy; and \tt{refactor-patches/\allowbreak{}phase3e/\allowbreak{}rebaseline\_record.md}
  classifying the \tt{set\_window} provenance repair as SC where A-19 classifies it CF
  \infd.
  \item \textbf{The audit--fix loop itself generates ungated change.} \vfy: fifteen repair
  commits landed between the two Claude passes, advancing the tip from \tt{1f40542} to
  \tt{e0d7e43}. Repair commits written outside a phase's gate profile are exactly the
  category of change the phase structure exists to prevent. It is possible to make the
  package worse by auditing it. \S12 therefore requires every closeout commit to carry a
  \S7.2 class and the corresponding gate profile.
\end{itemize}

\section{What must block 3f, and why that set is narrow}

\subsection{The blocker test}

An issue \textbf{blocks 3f} if it can change any of:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=3.2em,style=nextline,font=\normalfont\bfseries]
  \item[B1 --- Numerical result] the likelihood event term, the compensator, or the
  simulator.
  \item[B2 --- Accepted configuration space] which combinations of options construct and
  which are rejected, and with what named error.
  \item[B3 --- Public state transitions] the behaviour of constructor and
  \tt{set\_window}, including the failure path and its rollback.
  \item[B4 --- Provenance] the content of the provenance record, or its persistence
  through \tt{save\_rslts}.
  \item[B5 --- Validity of a verification gate] see \S3.2 below.
  \item[B6 --- The 3f freeze surface] anything 3f will freeze into typed configuration
  objects, i.e.\ the surface named by OP-3, OP-4, OP-7 and OP-13.
\end{description}

Everything else is non-blocking and goes to the ledger (\S5, R9) with an owning phase.
Non-blocking explicitly includes: editorial and historical documentation; diagnostic-only
defects that change no rejection behaviour; and defects reachable only through
configurations already rejected at the contract boundary --- provided the reachability
argument is \emph{recorded}, not assumed.

\subsection{B5 stated sharply: pin contamination}

3f is gated by deterministic trace equivalence and rectangular pins. That gate compares
the post-3f tip against a recorded baseline. It follows that

\begin{ruling}
\noindent\textbf{a defect on a path over which a 3f pin or trace baseline is taken is a
blocker even when the defect is otherwise benign}, because the equivalence gate will
faithfully certify the preservation of the defect and report success.
\end{ruling}

This is the clause most easily lost. It is why the blocking set is defined by
\emph{what 3f will pin and what 3f will freeze}, rather than by severity. A severe defect
that 3f preserves bit-identically and that 3g will repair does not block 3f; a mild defect
sitting inside a pinned path does.

B5 also covers the environment: pins and traces are meaningful only within the declared
environment (\tt{freeze-476c2a0/\allowbreak{}conda-env-illegal-dumping.yml}, plus machine
locality). A divergence between declared dependency metadata and the environment in which
gates actually run is a B5 blocker, not a housekeeping item.

\section{Proposed decisions}

\begin{ruling}
\noindent These are \emph{proposals} for the register, in its idiom. They are not settled
by this document being written.
\end{ruling}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=3.6em,style=nextline,font=\normalfont\bfseries]

\item[D-35 --- The 3f blocker test] An issue blocks the opening of Phase 3f if and only if
it can change the likelihood, the compensator, the simulator, the accepted configuration
space, a public state transition, provenance content or persistence, the validity of a
verification gate, or the surface 3f will freeze into typed configuration objects (clauses
B1--B6, \S3.1). \emph{Corollary, stated because it is easily lost}: a defect on a path over
which a 3f pin or trace baseline is taken is a blocker regardless of severity, because
deterministic trace equivalence would certify its preservation. Severity is not the
criterion; position relative to the pins and the freeze surface is.

\item[D-36 --- Finite readiness rule] Phase 3f opens when conditions R1--R9 of \S5 hold
simultaneously on one frozen candidate tip, and not otherwise. The rule is closed: no
condition of the form ``and no further defect is known'' appears in it. Findings that are
not blockers under D-35 do not gate 3f at any severity.

\item[D-37 --- Compatibility and state-transition matrix as a standing gate] The matrix of
\S6 is added to the \S12 acceptance matrix as a mandatory gate (\textbf{M}) for every
commit that changes the constructor signature, \tt{set\_window}, the trigger or support
selection logic, the cutoff interface, or provenance construction; and as a mandatory
exit gate (\textbf{E}) at the Phase 3 tip alongside D-20's Stage 3 SBC run. It does not
replace the pins, the SBC gates, or the polygon conservation tests; its sensitivity is
near-disjoint from all three.

\item[D-38 --- Coverage is recorded, absence of findings is not evidence] Two artifacts
become part of the development record and are maintained thereafter: a register-to-test
traceability matrix (\S7) and an audit coverage map (\S8). ``Unaudited'' becomes a
recorded state rather than an implicit claim, and a clean audit is interpretable only
against the coverage it declares.

\end{description}

\section{The readiness rule}

Phase 3f opens when all of the following hold on \emph{one frozen candidate tip}, recorded
by SHA. Conditions R1--R4 and R7--R9 follow the ChatGPT recommendation; R5, R6 and the
R2 RED requirement are additions from this document.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=3.0em,style=nextline,font=\normalfont\bfseries]
  \item[R1] Every known blocker under D-35 is fixed, or is shown unreachable with the
  reachability argument \emph{recorded in the ledger}, not merely asserted.
  \item[R2] Every fixed blocker has a focused regression test, and the test was
  demonstrated \textbf{RED at the pre-fix commit} before being made GREEN. A test written
  only after the fix does not discharge R2, because it does not establish that it detects
  the defect.
  \item[R3] The compatibility and state-transition matrix (\S6) passes on the candidate
  tip.
  \item[R4] The standing gates pass on the candidate tip: full suite; pins and traces for
  plain Hawkes, LGCP and full Cox--Hawkes; rectangle degeneracy of the two support modes;
  polygon numerical (\tabs) and conservation tests; end-to-end smoke fits for all three
  modes; lint; and the global JAX-state checks.
  \item[R5] The register-to-test traceability matrix (\S7) exists, and every decision
  \tt{D-1}\dots\tt{D-34+} and every \S11 cross-cutting invariant carries one of the four
  declared statuses. Gaps may remain \emph{registered}; they may not remain unnoticed.
  \item[R6] The audit coverage map (\S8) exists and no module in the 3f seam set carries
  status \tt{unaudited}.
  \item[R7] OP-3, OP-4, OP-7, OP-8 and OP-13 dispositions are recorded to the level of
  detail needed for 3f not to redesign them incidentally.
  \item[R8] One final bounded audit, scoped to the contracts of \S6--\S8 and to the
  proposed 3f seams, finds no new blocker. Termination rule in \S9.
  \item[R9] Every remaining non-blocking finding is entered in the ledger with class, date,
  and owning phase (3f, 3g, or post-Phase-3).
\end{description}

\section{The compatibility and state-transition matrix}

\subsection{Why a matrix rather than another review}

The defects cluster at \emph{combinations} of options, and combination space is where
example-based testing is systematically weakest: each individual option is exercised, no
pair is. A covering array converts an unbounded search into a finite, enumerable
obligation --- which is exactly the property ``audit until clean'' lacks.

\subsection{Separating configuration axes from assertion families}

The single most useful structural correction to the matrix as first proposed is to
separate \emph{what is varied} from \emph{what is asserted}. Provenance and leg
consistency are not axes of a configuration; they are properties that must hold at every
covered point. Conflating them inflates the array and leaves the assertions
under-specified.

\paragraph{Configuration axes (varied).}
\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.6em,style=nextline,font=\normalfont\bfseries]
  \item[C1 Model family] \{plain Hawkes, LGCP, full Cox--Hawkes\} --- 3 levels.
  \item[C2 Trigger specification] \{built-in Gaussian $\otimes$ exponential, power-law
  temporal, custom\} --- 3 levels.
  \fillanchor{enumerate the actual level set from the trigger classes in the code; do not
  take these three from this document}
  \item[C3 Excitation support mode] \{rectangle, polygon\} --- 2 levels.
  \item[C4 Cutoff input] \{tolerance-derived, physical override, omitted, explicit
  \tt{None}\} --- 4 levels.
  \item[C5 State path] \{constructor only, \tt{set\_window} success, \tt{set\_window}
  rejected\} --- 3 levels.
\end{description}

The full Cartesian product is $3\times3\times2\times4\times3 = 216$ points, of which a
substantial fraction is structurally degenerate (LGCP has no trigger, so C2 and C3
collapse). Pairwise coverage of the feasible space is achievable in roughly $12$--$20$
configurations --- the lower bound being $\max_{i\neq j}(|C_i|\cdot|C_j|) = 12$. Pairwise
coverage is the requirement, \emph{plus} every explicitly supported and every explicitly
rejected combination named in the register, which are added as forced rows regardless of
whether the covering array already contains them.

\paragraph{Assertion families (checked at every covered point).}
\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.6em,style=nextline,font=\normalfont\bfseries]
  \item[P1 Admissibility] the configuration either constructs, or raises a \emph{named}
  error whose message identifies the offending combination. A bare \tt{KeyError}, an
  \tt{AttributeError} from downstream, or silent acceptance all fail P1. Every rejection
  in the matrix is asserted by error type and by a message substring, not by
  \tt{pytest.raises(Exception)}.
  \item[P2 Leg consistency] the event term, the compensator, and the simulator consume the
  same support atoms and the same cutoff values (D-18; \S11.1). Assert by \emph{object
  identity} where the quantity is single-sourced --- the \tt{within\allowbreak{}\_real\allowbreak{}\_box\allowbreak{}\_window}
  pattern --- and by numerical agreement where it is not. Identity assertions are stronger
  and are preferred; a numerical assertion records that the leg is not yet single-sourced.
  \item[P3 Provenance] after any accepted transition, the changed axis is updated, the
  untouched axis is preserved \emph{bit-identically}, and the whole record round-trips
  through \tt{save\_rslts} and back.
  \item[P4 Transactionality] after a rejected transition, every public field, the
  excitation pair set, the support object, and the provenance record are exactly as they
  were before the call (A-14, D-33). Assert by whole-state comparison against a
  pre-call snapshot, not by spot-checking the fields the fix happened to touch.
  \item[P5 Numerical budget] in polygon mode \emph{at the defaults}, the mass evaluation
  meets A-21's production gate \tt{PRODUCTION\_TAU\_ABS}${}={}10^{-5}$ (D-25 path;
  not A-9's superseded $\tabs = 5.392\times10^{-4}$, which governs \tt{TAU\_DERIV} /
  OP-12 only --- see the Correction ruling below).
\end{description}

\subsection{P5 restated so the test is self-contained}

\begin{ruling}
\noindent\textbf{Correction.} An earlier draft of this section derived the mass-table
budget from the truncation mass and stated $\tabs = 5.3923032\times10^{-4}$. That is
wrong. \textbf{A-21 is authoritative: \tt{PRODUCTION\_TAU\_ABS} $=$ \tt{TAU\_ABS} $=
10^{-5}$}, carried in \tt{polygon\_mass.py}. The derivation below governs \tt{TAU\_DERIV}
(OP-12, open) and survives in code as \tt{LEGACY\_SHOOTOUT\_TAU\_ABS}. Using it as the
mass-table budget would relax the gate by roughly fifty times.
\end{ruling}

For the record, the superseded derivation: under the D-21 per-axis square cutoff the
omitted spatial mass is
\[
  \epsilon_s \;=\; 1 - \operatorname{erf}\!\left(\frac{w_s}{\sqrt{2}\,\sigma}\right)^{\!2},
\]
which at $w_s = 3\sigma$ gives $\epsilon_s = 5.3923032\times10^{-3}$; ten per cent of that
is $5.3923032\times10^{-4}$. The reasoning --- that numerical error should sit an order of
magnitude below error knowingly accepted by truncation --- remains sound, and is why that
value still governs the derivative gate.

The \vfy{} \tt{panel\_h\_m} finding is a violation of the \emph{production} budget
\emph{at the defaults}, and it survived because the gate was enforced when the backend was
chosen but never re-enforced against the shipped default configuration. P5 therefore tests
the defaults, not a hand-chosen panel height:
\[
  \max_j \bigl| M_j^{\text{table, defaults}}(\sigma) - M_j^{\text{reference}}(\sigma) \bigr|
  \;\le\; \text{\tt{PRODUCTION\_TAU\_ABS}}
\]
over the declared $\sigma$ range.

\begin{ruling}
\noindent\textbf{The assertion must measure, not proxy.} A panel-to-$\sigma$ ratio ceiling
is a resolution surrogate calibrated at one quadrature order; at a different \tt{gl\_order}
it admits tables outside budget while the error message names a tolerance nothing measured.
P5 compares against a higher-accuracy reference --- \tt{BUDGET\_REFERENCE\_GL\_ORDER} $=
32$ in host float64, oracle bound $10^{-6}$ --- at the table's \emph{actual} settings.
\end{ruling}

\subsection{Deliverable shape}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \tt{tests/test\allowbreak{}\_config\allowbreak{}\_matrix.py} --- the covering array, parameterized, one
  test function per assertion family, with the array itself defined as data at the top of
  the file so that a reader can see the covered points without reading the test bodies.
  \item \tt{tests/test\allowbreak{}\_state\allowbreak{}\_transitions.py} --- P3 and P4, which need
  snapshot helpers rather than parameterization.
  \item \tt{docs/config\allowbreak{}\_matrix.md} --- the axes, their level sets, the forced rows and
  why each is forced, and the covering-array generation method, so that the array can be
  regenerated rather than being an opaque literal.
\end{itemize}

\section{The final bounded audit and its termination rule}

R8 is a single audit, scoped and terminating.

\paragraph{Scope.} The \S6 contracts, the \S7 traceability gaps, the \S8 unaudited
modules within the 3f seam set, and the proposed 3f seams themselves. It is explicitly
\emph{not} another whole-package review, and findings outside D-35's blocker set do not
gate: they are logged and the audit continues.

\paragraph{Iteration.} Each iteration runs against a frozen candidate tip recorded by SHA.
If an iteration finds one or more blockers: fix them under \S12's commit discipline, rerun
the affected matrix rows and the R4 gates, re-freeze, and iterate.

\paragraph{Vary the search between iterations.} Because blind spots are correlated within
an auditor and a strategy, each iteration must record its auditor and its method, and
iteration $k+1$ must differ from iteration $k$ in at least one of the two. An iteration
that repeats both is not evidence.

\paragraph{Termination --- the part that prevents ``final audit'' from becoming the same
unbounded loop under another name.}

\begin{ruling}
\noindent The loop is capped at \textbf{three} iterations. An iteration that finds only
non-blockers terminates it. If the \emph{third} iteration still finds a blocker, the loop
stops and a scope review is held instead of a fourth pass. At that point the evidence no
longer supports the hypothesis ``one more fix is needed''; it supports ``the semantic
surface is not yet specified.'' The correct response is then re-specification --- which
may legitimately mean moving work into 3f as \emph{declared} semantic change under \S7.2,
rather than pretending it is behaviour-preserving --- not a further audit.
\end{ruling}

\subsection{The real-unit trigger contract}\label{sec:realunit-trigger}

The internal rescaling of \S\ref{sec:precision} is a per-axis affine map,
and a per-axis map does not commute with isotropy: a kernel declared
isotropic in internal coordinates is anisotropic in real coordinates with
axis ratio $L_x/L_y$, the aspect ratio of the bounding rectangle. For the
spatial trigger this elevated a numerical convention into a modelling
commitment --- the fitted kernel \emph{family} depended on a preprocessing
choice --- which is category~(c) behaviour smuggled in through a
category~(a) device.

The specification therefore fixes the contract at the model level: the
spatial trigger is a \emph{real-unit object}. Offspring displacements are
$N(0,\sigma_x^2 I_2)$ in the units of the input coordinates; $\sigma_x^2$
and its prior are real-unit quantities (there is no default prior --- the
user must supply one, now stateable in physical units); the spatial window
$w_s$ is a real length, and the truncation region is a square \emph{in real
space}. Internally the likelihood still integrates against the internal
measure, so the unit boundary consists of exactly three declared
conversions, single-sourced in the implementation:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item the event term stretches internal pair displacements per axis and
        converts the real-area density to the internal measure by the
        Jacobian $L_x L_y$ of the affine map,
        $\varphi_{\mathrm{int}} = L_x L_y\, \varphi_{\mathrm{real}}$;
  \item the compensator stretches its per-axis rectangle limits to real
        units (its spatial factor is a probability, hence dimensionless ---
        no Jacobian);
  \item the simulator draws the displacement directly in real units.
\end{enumerate}
On a square box of side $L$ the Jacobian cancels the density normalization
exactly, so $\sigma_{\mathrm{real}} = \sigma_{\mathrm{int}} L$ reproduces
the historical kernel --- the backward-equivalence pin. The temporal trigger
($\beta$, $w$) deliberately remains internal-unit: its conversion is a pure
relabel (category~(a) with no family content), deferred to the conversion
layer, and the asymmetry is declared rather than hidden.

Two identities pin the contract. The unit-invariance identity (I10) is
\emph{restated as covariance}: the log-likelihood is preserved under
similarity transformations of the spatial inputs with
$\sigma_x^2 \mapsto c^2 \sigma_x^2$, while the internal representation
remains invariant under any axis-wise affine map; the old identity ---
fixed-parameter invariance under \emph{non-uniform} rescaling --- is now
asserted to \emph{fail}, since that invariance was precisely the defect. A
new identity (I12, box invariance) states the operational content: one
real-coordinate dataset ingested under two differently shaped bounding
rectangles must agree on the excitation pair set (under a real-unit $w_s$),
the real-unit displacements and kernel densities, the truncated compensator
(when the $w_s$-square around every event is interior to both rectangles),
and, under a shared seed, the offspring cascades byte-for-byte.

One boundary condition survives at the data contract: the input coordinates
must be metric. In geographic coordinates one degree of longitude is
$\cos(\mathrm{lat})$ degrees of latitude on the ground, so a
degree-isotropic kernel is ground-anisotropic --- the defect re-entering
through the front door. Ingestion warns heuristically on degree-like
domains.

\subsection{Stretching and the background: likelihood-exact, prior-inexact}
\label{sec:background-stretch}

It is worth recording why the background needed no analogous correction.
The distortion of \S\ref{sec:realunit-trigger} attaches to \emph{metric}
objects --- quantities defined through distances or densities in space. The
background's likelihood legs contain none: the fields are piecewise constant
on their partitions, the event term is a cell-membership lookup (invariant
under any affine reparametrization applied consistently to events and
grid), and each compensator is a sum of cell values against cell measures.
Stretch the bounding rectangle however one likes and the discrete model's
background likelihood is literally the same function of the same field
values --- the category~(a) claim holds exactly here.

The caveat sits entirely on the \emph{prior} side. The spatial PriorVAE
decoder was trained to emulate a GP with an \emph{isotropic}
squared-exponential kernel on the \emph{internal} unit square
($\ell \sim \mathrm{InvGamma}(15,1)$). Under the affine map the prior's
real-space correlation structure is therefore anisotropic in exactly the way
the trigger's kernel was: correlation length $\ell L_x$ east--west against
$\ell L_y$ north--south, and cell resolution $L_x/25 \times L_y/25$ per
axis. The geometry did not disappear; it was baked in at decoder-training
time and now lives wholly in the prior.

The reason this is a flag rather than a defect is \emph{where} the geometry
binds. For the trigger the anisotropy was structural in the likelihood: one
shape parameter, a fixed imposed ratio, no amount of data able to undo it.
For the background the field carries hundreds of effective degrees of
freedom and the anisotropic prior acts only as a regularizer: the data
overwhelm it wherever events are dense, its influence vanishes
asymptotically at fixed grid, and its visible effect is directionally uneven
smoothing in sparse regions. It is distinct from --- though compounding ---
the PriorVAE oversmoothing already documented (prior-side variance
shrinkage, a property of the surrogate rather than of any geometry). The
one-dimensional temporal and seasonal fields rescale by a single factor each
and raise no shape question. Repairing the spatial prior's geometry would
require retraining the decoder against the real-space aspect ratio of each
domain --- coupling the pretrained artifact to the data --- and is therefore
documented rather than patched; if per-park fits ever show boundary-region
artifacts aligned with the box axes, this is the first place to look.
